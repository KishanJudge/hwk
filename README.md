# Thatcher and the IRA - Dealing with Terror

**1. When was the Anglo-Irish Agreement signed?**
15 November 1985, at Hillsborough Castle.

**2. Who was involved?**
Margaret Thatcher and Dr Garret FitzGerald (Irish Taoiseach) signed it. The negotiators were Robert Armstrong and David Goodall for Britain and Michael Lillis for Ireland. Douglas Hurd, Geoffrey Howe and Tom King (Northern Ireland Secretary) were also involved.

**3. Who was furious?**
Unionists. They were already gathering at the gates of Hillsborough Castle, because they had been excluded from the agreement.

**4. Who was the chief negotiator?**
Robert Armstrong, Thatcher's lead negotiator for two years and the chief architect of the Agreement.

**5. Background (include information about Neave):**
- Thatcher defeated Ted Heath for the Conservative leadership, and became Prime Minister in 1979.
- Airey Neave organised her leadership campaign and was her spokesman on Northern Ireland, "the prism through which she viewed the troubles".
- Neave believed there had to be a military solution before a political one: "an army that's winning needs no recruiting sergeant".
- He had escaped from Colditz. Thatcher had seen her hometown bombed in the war, which shaped her thinking on Northern Ireland.
- Neave saw Northern Ireland as an integral part of the UK. He was murdered in a Republican bomb attack at the House of Commons just weeks before she became PM, so she lost a close friend and her chief adviser on Northern Ireland.
- After his death, his opinions became her policies.

**6. What was Thatcher's view from 1979 onwards in terms of Northern Ireland?**
She saw it as a security problem for the military and police to deal with. She wanted it contained and had little affection or interest in the place. She was a unionist in principle but found dealing with unionist leaders uphill work. She often compared the Republic's claim on Northern Ireland to Hitler's claim on the Sudetenland, and she was happier talking to soldiers.

**7. Detail the IRA attacks in 1979 and their impact:**
- Neave was killed in a bomb attack at the House of Commons, weeks before she became PM.
- In August 1979 the IRA killed Lord Mountbatten and 18 soldiers in separate attacks.
- The impact was that Mountbatten's death made her see the problem "basically in security terms".
- It caused her to review her military strategy, the first of many such reviews.
- She demanded greater security cooperation from Irish Premier Jack Lynch (helicopter overflights and extradition). The tense meeting hurt Lynch politically, and he resigned weeks later.

**8. What happened in December 1980?**
Thatcher met Charles Haughey at a summit in Dublin Castle with a very high-ranking delegation (including Carrington and Howe). Haughey wanted an international conference of all parties, so the "political romance" was all but over. IRA prisoners at Long Kesh had begun a hunger strike, which was still on during the summit.

**9. What impact might the first IRA hunger strike have had on Thatcher?**
It may have pushed her towards agreeing what was then called an Anglo-Irish framework. The delegation was sent partly to defuse the hunger strike situation.

**10. What was initially believed regarding Thatcher and the first hunger strike?**
It was thought for a time that her government had given in to the prisoners' demands, but she hadn't.

**11. Second hunger strike:**
It began in March 1981 and led to the death of Bobby Sands and nine other Republican prisoners. Almost seventy people were murdered during the seven-month dispute. Thatcher said there would be "no political status" and "we will not compromise". Her defenders said the IRA high command starved the men, not her. The government found it hard to find a way out once it started, and she was "not easily shaken" once set on a course.

**12. What did Thatcher do secretly?**
She negotiated with the Republican leadership during the hunger strike. Her own handwriting could be seen on a message sent directly to the prisoners, and she was prepared to deal with offers and compromises. It was the first time she had negotiated with Republicans. She later used secret back channels, which ultimately led to the IRA ceasefire.

**13. How did the hunger strikes impact upon the IRA?**
After the strike, Thatcher conceded better conditions to the prisoners. The IRA was rejuvenated and Republicans built a political base. It led to a huge surge of support for the Republican movement and the growth of its political wing, an "unintended consequence".

**14. Haughey's actions during Falklands conflict and the result:**
In 1982 Ireland held a seat on the UN Security Council, and Haughey used it to try to bring international pressure on Britain. The result was that Thatcher became sure Haughey was not to be trusted and was no friend of Britain. Anglo-Irish relations broke down, and his initiative ended any possibility of dialogue.

**15. Gerry Adams:**
The Sinn Féin leader was elected in West Belfast in the 1983 election, consolidating Republicans' move into constitutional politics.

**16. Sinn Fein:**
Its support grew after the hunger strikes, which Sinn Féin treats as its "year zero". The "alienation of the minority" that FitzGerald worried about meant growth in support for Sinn Féin. Dublin and London both wanted to prevent its resurgence in the Irish political system.

**17. How did Fitzgerald change his approach and what was the impact?**
- After Thatcher ignored his invitation to talks, he appealed to her only real interest in Northern Ireland, security.
- Michael Lillis approached his London counterpart on a walk along the canal and suggested they work together on security, "not for an Irish political agenda".
- The impact was that Thatcher agreed to open talks, but kept the Northern Ireland Office out. She gave the job to David Goodall and Robert Armstrong, her chief security advisers.

**18. Brighton Party Conference and impact:**
In October 1984 the IRA bombed the Grand Hotel during the Conservative conference, killing five and injuring 31. Thatcher was the IRA's number one target. It hardened her resolve ("This government will not weaken"), and the bomb didn't wreck the negotiations.

**19. US involvement:**
- After Thatcher publicly rejected the New Ireland Forum's three options ("out, out, out"), FitzGerald turned to Reagan and Irish-American Tip O'Neill.
- O'Neill wrote to Reagan, pressing him to raise the breakdown in talks with Thatcher, and Reagan asked her several times to go ahead.
- Thatcher's Washington visits (December 1984 and February 1985) gave a chance to apply pressure.
- Thatcher later said "it was the Americans who made me do it".

**20. Draft Anglo-Irish Agreement:**
Armstrong met his Dublin counterparts for two years, and the drafting was complete by autumn 1985. Weeks before the summit, Thatcher replaced Douglas Hurd with Tom King, who thought the draft unfair to unionists. Armstrong, Hurd and Howe helped overcome his concerns, since it "had gone so far by then there was no going back".

**21. November 15th, Hillsborough Castle:**
Thatcher flew in by RAF helicopter. On arrival she went upstairs to phone Ian Gow, a close friend who was resigning as a minister in protest. She and FitzGerald signed, and the agreement said Northern Ireland's status would only change with the consent of a majority there. It also gave the Republic a voice in a conference on stated aspects of Northern Ireland affairs.

**22. Unionists' reaction:**
They felt betrayal: "Why didn't she discuss this with us?" They had been left in the dark, which was deliberate because there would be no agreement if they were consulted. All 15 unionist MPs resigned their seats. Over 100,000 people attended a protest rally at Belfast City Hall. One said they were "dreadfully angry to the point that we almost lost faith in the democratic process".

**23. How did Thatcher feel about the Anglo-Irish Agreement?**
She regretted it almost from the moment of Gow's resignation. It went "against the grain" and she half regretted it. She had expected it to act like a magic wand, improving security and sidelining the Provisionals. She blamed FitzGerald for its failings, and later dismissed it and backed Enoch Powell's rejection of it.

**24. What was the impact of the Agreement?**
It did not end the violence. The SAS killed eight IRA members at Loughgall, and the joint approach gave the security forces a freer hand. In August 1988 eight soldiers died in an IRA attack near Ballygawley. More than a thousand people died during her time in office. It did, however, start the gradual evolution of the peace process, as secret channels reopened in the late 1980s.

**25. Thatcher and the IRA:**
- She cultivated an Iron Lady image but secretly negotiated with the IRA.
- She communicated with the Republican leadership during the hunger strike.
- Messages were exchanged with the IRA in the years after the Anglo-Irish Agreement, according to Tom King (Northern Ireland Secretary 1985-90). Nicholas Scott alerted the Irish in 1986 that London was in contact with the Provisionals.
- The first acknowledged face-to-face contact came in October 1990, with Martin McGuinness (the transcript says "Mark McGinnis").
- She left office in November 1990. The channels she opened "just may have begun the process that eventually led to peace".
